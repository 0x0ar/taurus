taurus-audio

Taurus ships its own small audio processor, taurus-audio. It replaces EasyEffects and depends only on PipeWire. The equalizer, balance, mono, channel swap, dynamic range modes and the dynamic EQ in the music popup all run on it.

It creates a virtual output called Taurus, processes everything sent to it and plays the result on your real output device. The shell talks to it over a local socket, so changing a slider is instant.

In the shell​

  • taurusd start starts the engine, so it runs whenever the shell does.
  • Your real device stays the default output. The engine moves each app's audio onto the Taurus output, processes it and plays it on the default device, just as EasyEffects did. The Taurus output appears in the volume popup next to your devices.
  • Choosing a device in the volume popup moves the processed audio to that device. The volume keys and every app that asks for the default output see your real device.
  • An app you move to another device yourself, for example in pavucontrol, stays there and skips the engine. Apps that pick their own device are left alone too.
  • When the engine stops, apps go back to the default device.
  • Settings survive a reboot. The engine keeps them in ~/.local/state/taurus/audio.state, and the music popup reads them back from there.

Voice chat apps​

Voice chat apps skip the engine and play directly on your device, so their sound is not processed. Without this, the people in your call would hear their own voices when you share your screen with audio: the share leaves out the voice chat app, but the engine's processed mix contains its sound too, so it would be captured and sent back to them.

These apps are skipped:

  • Discord, including PTB and Canary
  • Vesktop, Equibop, Legcord, ArmCord and WebCord
  • Zoom
  • Teams for Linux
  • any app that marks its playback as call audio

Everything these apps play skips the engine, including music and videos in them, so the equalizer and the dynamics modes do not apply to it. If you want an app processed anyway, move it to the Taurus output in pavucontrol. Each stream is checked only once, when it starts, so nothing switches devices in the middle of playback.

Calls in Telegram and in a browser are still processed, because their call audio looks the same as the rest of their sound.

Dynamic EQ​

The Dynamic button on the equalizer page turns on an adaptive tonal balance. It listens to the last few seconds of audio and gently moves the balance toward the typical balance of commercial recordings, without changing loudness. It replaces the manual equalizer while it is on, so the sliders and presets are dimmed until you turn it off.

The design and the research behind each number are described in the taurus-audio README.

Visualizer​

The bar, widget, lock screen and music popup visualizers read their spectrum from taurus-audio visualizer, a port of cava's processing, so cava is no longer needed. The shell starts it only while music plays and a visualizer is on screen, and stops it shortly after the music stops. It runs separately from the engine, so the visualizer also works when the engine is off. The options are listed in the taurus-audio README.

Installation​

Installing and updating​

The install script downloads the latest release for x86_64 or aarch64, checks it against the published checksums and installs it to /usr/local/bin. Prebuilt binaries need glibc 2.39 or newer. On other systems, such as Void Linux musl, the installer installs a compiler and the PipeWire development files and builds it from source. Running the installer again updates it.

The Arch packages bundle a release, and the Nix flake builds it from source.

Moving from EasyEffects​

The first time the installer or the shell runs with the audio engine installed, Taurus undoes what it set up for EasyEffects. This runs once:

  • stops the EasyEffects instance that the old equalizer started, the one running the live_eq preset, and ee-eq.service if it is running
  • removes the live_eq preset that Taurus used to generate
  • removes the systemctl --user enable --now easyeffects line from the autostart file Taurus installed for Hyprland, Niri, Sway or Mango, keeping a .pre-audio-engine.bak copy of each file it edits

Anything you set up yourself stays as it is. EasyEffects stays installed, and your presets, services and autostart entries are not touched. An EasyEffects you started yourself keeps running. A line you moved or changed, and config files that are links, for example from Home Manager or a dotfiles manager, are left alone too.

If EasyEffects is still running or set to start at login after this, Taurus tells you once: in the installer output, or as a notification when the shell starts. EasyEffects can interfere with the audio engine, so turn it off unless you use it for something else, such as microphone effects, which Taurus does not do. Depending on how you set it up, that means systemctl --user disable --now easyeffects, turning off its start-at-login option in EasyEffects, or removing services.easyeffects.enable from your Home Manager configuration.
